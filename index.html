01

Philosophy

I consider myself quite late to the investment game. I bought my first stock at 25, after leaving my first job, and did all the things value investors advise against: bought trendy stocks, speculated on short-term rises, and sold sporadically. I had entered the market during the COVID investing boom, and by 2022 my losses had reached five figures.

It took me years to recover from those losses before I started investing again. For three months, I wholeheartedly studied Charlie Munger and Warren Buffett and religiously read every post on Michael Burry's Substack. Slowly, I began forming a framework of my own while building the discipline that value investing demands.

  1. i.

    The power of compounding

    A child who saves $3 a day from age 10 would have about $30k by age 30 at a 3% annual return. Conversely, spending $30k today means forgoing roughly $24k in additional earnings over 20 years at the same rate. We tend to overlook what happens in the distant future; working out the math on a spreadsheet makes it impossible to ignore.

    → Run the numbers yourself
  2. ii.

    Invest in durable businesses

    After heavy losses trading growth stocks in 2022, I learned the hard way to evaluate a business and its financial performance thoroughly, and to buy only with a margin of safety. Businesses that generate cash while needing little or no additional capital trump everything else.

    → Value a business
  3. iii.

    Think like an owner

    When you invest, you own a stake in the company, so put yourself in the owner's shoes. Before calling yourself a shareholder, study the moat, the financial performance and the quality of management. It matters for your returns, and it's also quite fun.
